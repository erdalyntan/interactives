Finding the Line of Intersection between two non-parallel Planes

Problem Definition

Given two non-parallel planes \( \mathit{p}_1 \) and \( \mathit{p}_2 \), they intersect along a unique line \( \mathit{l} \). To find the equation of this line, we need a point that lies on both planes and a direction vector that is perpendicular to both normal vectors.

Step 1

Identify Normals

Extract normal vectors \( \mathbf{\mathit{n}}_1 \) and \( \mathbf{\mathit{n}}_2 \) from the equations.

Step 2

Cross Product

The direction vector of the line is \( \mathbf{\mathit{d}} = \mathbf{\mathit{n}}_1 \times \mathbf{\mathit{n}}_2 \).

Step 3

Solve for a Point

Set one variable (e.g., \( z=0 \)) and solve simultaneously to find point \( \mathbf{\mathit{a}} \).

Step 4

Assemble Equation

Final equation: \( \mathit{l}: \mathbf{\mathit{r}} = \mathbf{\mathit{a}} + \lambda \mathbf{\mathit{d}}, \lambda \in \mathbb{R} \).
